[This headline](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/3/12/irans-president-sets-terms-to-end-the-war-is-an-off-ramp-in-sight) today about ending the war is a perfect example of the media environment we’re in. Headlines, having the ability to calm the markets, are a vital instrument for the US in this war. The markets stopped listening to Trump last week. The new meme going around is that one should only listen to news about diplomacy if it comes from the Iranians. Therefore, in order to calm the markets, the US needed a headline saying that Iran itself said they want to end the war. And that is what we got. 

But when you dive in, what do we learn is the substance of these comments? The president of Iran spoke with the head of the EU and said Iran has “necessary will [to end the conflict] provided that essential conditions are met, especially the guarantees required to prevent repetition of the aggression."

This is no different than the position articulated weeks ago when they rejected the US's 15 points. They will accept an end to the war when their demands are met, and those demands include permanent security guarantees against future aggression, as well as reparations and sovereignty over the Strait of Hormuz, none of which the US and Israel will consent to.

The Iranian president’s comments were not new, but the western aligned press were willing to frame them in such a way that it would boost stocks, reduce 10Y yield, reduce the VIX, and reduce the price of oil—all of which are the mechanism Iran uses to hurt the US. In the economic war against Iran, headlines are one of the United States' most powerful asset. For exactly that reason, they are totally unreliable, and find a way to stretch the truth further every day.
